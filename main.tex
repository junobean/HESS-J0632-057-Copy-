\section{Introduction}
HESS J0632+057 was discovered by H.E.S.S. as a point-like very-high-energy (VHE) gamma-ray source in the Monoceros region~\citep{Aharonian2007}. Its position coincides with the Be star HD 259440 (MWC 148), and the variability of its X-ray counterpart led to the proposal that the system is a gamma-ray binary~\citep{Hinton2009}. Optical spectroscopy classifies HD 259440 as a B0pe star with a mass of 13.2--19.0~$M_\odot$ at a distance of 1.1--1.7~kpc~\citep{Aragona2010}.

The binary nature of HESS J0632+057 was confirmed by the detection of an orbital period in the Swift-XRT light curve, first measured as $321 \pm 5$~days~\citep{Bongiorno2011} and later refined to $315^{+6}_{-4}$~days~\citep{Aliu2014} and $313^{+11}_{-8}$~days~\citep{Moritani2018}. A joint analysis of the H.E.S.S., MAGIC, and VERITAS data yielded a period of $\sim$317~days~\citep{Adams2021}, which is adopted in this analysis. The X-ray and VHE light curves show a sharp primary outburst and a broader secondary outburst, separated by a faint phase in which the source is barely detected, and the VHE emission is modulated in phase with the X-ray emission~\citep{Aliu2014, Adams2021, Moritani2018}.

Despite these observations, the geometry of the system remains uncertain. The radial velocities of the Be star led to two different orbital solutions: a highly eccentric orbit ($e = 0.83$) by Casares et al.~\citep{Casares2012} and a less eccentric orbit ($e \approx 0.6$) with the periastron on the opposite side of the orbit by Moritani et al.~\citep{Moritani2018}. The nature of the compact object is also unknown, although the small mass function obtained by Moritani et al.\ suggests a neutron star.

In this note, we present the analysis of HESS J0632+057 with $\sim$2886 days of HAWC Pass 5 data. Exploiting the continuous coverage of all orbital phases and the sensitivity of HAWC above 10~TeV, we measure the spectrum of HESS J0632+057 in four orbital-phase intervals and study its orbital modulation at the highest energies.

\section{Map-making} 
To study the orbital-phase dependence of HESS J0632+057, we divide the HAWC data into four orbital-phase intervals based on previous IACT and X-ray observations~\citep{Bongiorno2011, Aliu2014, Adams2021}. We adopt an orbital period of 317 days~\citep{Adams2021} and define phase zero as MJD 54857.0, corresponding to the date of the first Swift-XRT observation~\citep{Bongiorno2011}.
The four orbital states are defined as 

\begin{itemize}
    \item First high state:
    $\phi = 0.2$--$0.4$,

    \item Low state:
    $\phi = 0.4$--$0.6$,

    \item Second high state:
    $\phi = 0.6$--$0.8$,

    \item Medium state:
    $\phi = 0.8$--$0.2$.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Map-making Procedure}
The reconstructed HAWC data, namely reduced dataset, are stored in individual chunks. We divide the Pass 5 reduced dataset according to the orbital phase of HESS J0632+057. Chunks 1-102 are excluded due to poor agreement between data and Monte Carlo simulations. After applying the phase selections, raw maps are generated separately for each chunk. Each reduced data file contains reconstructed event information, including the shower direction, core position, GPS time, and other event-level quantities. These quantities are used to assign the selected events to the corresponding pixels of the skymap. The Pass 5 binning scheme is applied during this step, resulting in a separate raw map for each bin. Because of computational and constraints, the raw maps are generated separately for individual chunks. The chunks-level raw maps are subsequently combined for each orbital state and Pass 5 bin to construct the phase-folded maps. After the chunk-level maps are combined, two additional process are applied: randomized background estimation and rejiggering.
The randomized background procedure is applied ti the high-energy bins, where limited event statistics can lead to unstable background estimates with the standard direct integration method. The final step is rejiggering, which smooths the background map on an angular scale of $0.5^\circ$. All scripts used for the map-making procedure can be found here: \href{https://gitlab.com/hawc-observatory/sandboxes/kyoungjh}{\texttt{map-making codes}}

\subsection{Sanity Check with Crab}
As a sanity check, we analyze the Crab Nebula in each phase-folded map. The Crab Nebula provides a stable reference source for validating the map-making procedure. It is modeled as a point source with a log parabola spectrum, following previous HAWC measurements of the Crab Nebula~\citep{Abeysekara2019, Albert2024}. Figure~\ref{fig:crab_sed} shows the SED of the Crab Nebula, and Table~\ref{tab:crab_fit} summarizes the best-fit spectral parameters obtained from each phase-folded map and the combined map. \section{Baseline Modeling} 
The baseline modeling is performed using the combined map. The likelihood fits in this analysis are carried out with the 3ML framework~\citep{Vianello2015} and the HAL plugin. The baseline analysis consists of two main steps: a systematic source search pipeline, followed by morphological and spectral modeling. Figure~\ref{fig:Combined_sigmap} shows the ROI.

\subsection{Systematic Source Search}
\label{sec:source_search}
To identify the gamma-ray sources in the ROI without prior assumptions, we apply a systematic source search to the combined map. The search starts from a background-only model; diffuse Galactic emission is not included. Candidate sources are modeled as point sources with a power-law spectrum with an index of $-2.6$ and a pivot energy of 7~TeV. The search proceeds in two stages.

\paragraph{Point source test.}
In each step, a point source is added at the position of the most significant residual excess, and all free parameters of the model are refitted. The new source is kept if it improves the test statistic by $\Delta\mathrm{TS} \geq 25$, and the procedure is repeated until no further source passes this threshold. The first two steps add point sources with $\Delta\mathrm{TS} = 48$ and 29, respectively. The source candidate in the third step gives $\Delta\mathrm{TS} = 14$ and is rejected, so the point source test results in two sources.

\paragraph{Extension and curvature test.}
For each source, we then test whether an extended morphology (Gaussian on the sphere) or a curved spectrum (log parabola) is preferred over the point-like morphology and the power-law spectrum. A test is accepted if it improves the fit by $\Delta\mathrm{TS} \geq 16$. The results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:source_search_tests}. Only the extension of Source~2 passes the threshold. The extension of Source~1 ($\Delta\mathrm{TS} = 15.9$) is marginally below the threshold.

\subsection{Pivot Energy}
\label{sec:pivot}
The flux normalization $K$ and the photon index of a power-law spectrum are correlated unless $K$ is defined at the decorrelation energy of the measurement. To minimize this correlation, we scan the pivot energy from 1 to 60~TeV in steps of 1~TeV with the baseline model and compute the correlation coefficient $\rho(K,\mathrm{index})$ for each source. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pivot_scan}, $|\rho|$ is minimized at 5~TeV for HESS J0632+057 (Source~1) and at 7~TeV for HAWC J0635+070 (Source~2). We therefore adopt pivot energies of 5~TeV and 7~TeV for the two sources in all subsequent fits, including the phase-dependent analysis and the systematic studies. The pivot energy of 7~TeV used in the systematic source search (Section~\ref{sec:source_search}) was chosen before this optimization.

\section{Phase-dependent Analysis}
\label{sec:phase}
The baseline source model derived from the combined map is used as the starting point for the phase-dependent analysis. The four phase-folded maps corresponding to the first high, low, second high, and medium states are analyzed separately. Figure~\ref{tab:Sgi_map_phase_folded} shows the pre-trial significance maps for each orbital state. 
The spectral properties of HESS J0632+057 are then compared among the four orbital states to investigate possible orbital modulation of the VHE gamma-ray emission.
In the phase-dependent analysis, the spectral and morphological parameters of HAWC J0635+070 are fixed to the best-fit values obtained from the combined map. Since the phase-folded maps are constructed according to the orbital phase of HESS J0632+057, HAWC J0635+070 is assumed to remain unchanged among the four orbital states. In addition, the morphological parameters of HESS J0632+057 are fixed to the values obtained from the combined-map analysis because of the limited statistics in the individual phase-folded maps.
Table~\ref{tab:best-fit_result_phase_folded} summarizes the best-fit results for each orbital state.

\section{Systematic studies}
Systematic uncertainties of the free parameters in the final model are accounted for by varying the detector response files. The likelihood fit is repeated with each alternative detector response file, while the same map, ROI, bin list, and source model as in the nominal analysis are used. The detector response files used to study the systematic uncertainties are named with the following tags, grouped into four systematic sources:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
    \item Broadpulse: \texttt{broadpulse-\char`\{0p00,0p50,0p75,1p25\char`\}},
    \item Charge uncertainty: \texttt{charge-uncertainty-0p00}, \texttt{daqsim-config\_charge-uncertainty-0p06},
    \item PMT threshold: \texttt{daqsim-config\_threshold-\char`\{0p15,0p25\char`\}},
    \item Detector epoch: \texttt{run\_\char`\{4255,5214,5689,6283,6801,7578\char`\}}.
\end{itemize}

For each systematic source, the largest positive and the largest negative shifts of the best-fit value relative to the nominal result are evaluated. The total detector-response systematic uncertainty is obtained by adding the larger of the two shifts of each source in quadrature. To be conservative, the total is represented with $\pm$, regardless of the direction of the individual shifts. This procedure is applied to the combined map and to each of the four phase-folded maps. The resulting systematic uncertainties of the free parameters are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:systematic}. In the combined map, these are the position, flux normalization $K$, and photon index of HESS J0632+057 and the position, diffusion radius, $K$, photon index, and cutoff energy of HAWC J0635+070. In the phase-folded maps, only $K$ and the photon index of HESS J0632+057 are free.

\section{Discussions}

\subsection{Orbital Solution}
\label{sec:orbit}
The orbital parameters of HESS J0632+057 are still under debate. We consider two representative orbital solutions derived from optical spectroscopy of the Be companion, those of Casares et al.~\citep{Casares2012} and Moritani et al.~\citep{Moritani2018}, and examine where the four orbital-phase intervals used in this analysis are located on each orbit.

The orbital parameters of the two solutions are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:orbit}. Casares et al.\ derived a highly eccentric orbit ($e = 0.83$) from the radial velocities of the absorption lines, folded with the 321-day period of Bongiorno et al.~\citep{Bongiorno2011}. Moritani et al.\ measured the radial velocities from the wings of the H$\alpha$ emission line and obtained a less eccentric orbit ($e = 0.64$) with the periastron on the opposite side of the orbit, adopting the 313-day period derived from the Swift-XRT light curve. Both works define phase zero at JD 2454857.5 (MJD 54857.0), which is the same phase zero used in this analysis.

Figure~\ref{fig:orbit} shows the orbit of the compact object relative to the Be star for each solution, with the four orbital-phase intervals of this analysis. The two solutions place the HAWC orbital states at very different positions:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
    \item In the Casares et al.\ solution, periastron and both conjunctions fall within the medium state ($\phi = 0.8$--$0.2$), and apastron falls within the low state. The first and second high states occur before and after apastron, respectively, where the compact object is far from the Be star ($r > 1.2a$).
    \item In the Moritani et al.\ solution, periastron, which almost coincides with the superior conjunction, falls within the second high state, and apastron, which almost coincides with the inferior conjunction, falls within the medium state. The first high state occurs after apastron, and the low state covers the approach to periastron.
\end{itemize}

The orbital periods adopted in the two solutions (321 and 313 days) differ from the 317-day period used to fold the HAWC data. Here we adopt the published periastron phases with the common phase zero. Because the difference in period accumulates over the $\sim$10 orbital cycles covered by the HAWC data, the orbital positions in Figure~\ref{fig:orbit} should be regarded as approximate.

\subsection{HAWC Energy Range}
\label{sec:energy_range}
HAWC is sensitive to gamma rays up to energies beyond the range covered by the IACT observations of HESS J0632+057, providing a complementary view of the high-energy end of the spectrum.

To quantify the energy range over which HESS J0632+057 is detected, we multiply the best-fit power-law spectrum of each state by a step function. For the minimum energy, the step function removes the emission below a cut energy $E_{\mathrm{cut}}$; for the maximum energy, it removes the emission above $E_{\mathrm{cut}}$. The spectral parameters of HESS J0632+057 and the model of HAWC J0635+070 are fixed to their best-fit values, and only $E_{\mathrm{cut}}$ is scanned. The minimum (maximum) energy is defined as the value of $E_{\mathrm{cut}}$ at which the fit becomes worse than the best-fit model without the step function by $\Delta\mathrm{TS} = 2\Delta(-\log L) = 1$, i.e.\ at the $1\sigma$ level. This follows the definition used in the HAWC analysis note on LS 5039. The resulting energy ranges are listed in Table~\ref{tab:energy_range}.
